## Overview of OctoAcme project management processes

OctoAcme follows a customer-first, iterative project lifecycle that begins with initiation and ends with reflection and improvement. Teams first validate the problem, identify stakeholders, define success metrics, and confirm the business case before moving into planning. The project initiation process produces a lightweight one-pager with goals, milestones, risks, and resource needs so the team can decide whether to approve or pause the work. Once approved, the project moves into planning, where the team creates a prioritized backlog, defines acceptance criteria, estimates effort, identifies dependencies, and aligns on the release plan and definition of done.

The program relies on clear role ownership and cross-functional collaboration. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize the work, project managers coordinate schedules, risks, communications, and dependencies, developers implement and test features, QA validates acceptance and quality, and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. These roles are reinforced by core project artifacts such as the project charter, roadmap, risk register, backlog, acceptance criteria, and retrospective notes. Together, these artifacts create a shared source of truth across the project and help maintain alignment between delivery, leadership, and stakeholders.

Communication is a routine part of execution rather than a separate activity. Daily standups surface progress, blockers, and dependencies, while weekly project and product syncs review status, risks, and decisions. Stakeholders receive milestone-based updates and status reports, and the team escalates unresolved issues through a structured path from the delivery team to the PM, product lead, and sponsor when needed. Risk and dependency management are tracked continuously so the team can take corrective action early, and the project board and documentation remain the central tools for visibility and shared context.

Quality and release management are built into the workflow from start to finish. The team emphasizes automated testing in CI, security scanning, unit and integration coverage, and smoke testing for critical flows before production release. Execution practices include PR review standards, acceptance criteria checks, operational tracking, and regular demos or reviews to maintain momentum. Each release requires validation of acceptance criteria, deployment readiness, and rollback planning, while retrospectives and action tracking ensure each project learns from its wins and mistakes. This creates a disciplined but adaptable model that balances accountability, quality, and continuous improvement.
